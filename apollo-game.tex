\section*{Start here: a five-minute crew briefing}
\begin{panel}{Equipment and session length}
Print the ten cards for your mission and its ledger. Bring pencils,
erasers, scrap paper, and at least one physical slide rule with C/D and
A scales. An L scale is required for Apollo 13's PC+2 problem; a referee
may supply a printed logarithmic scale if the rule lacks L.
S/T scales are optional because a small tangent table is supplied.
No electronic calculators during play.
Allow 90--150 minutes for Apollo 11, or 2--3 hours for Apollo 13.
\end{panel}

\textbf{Choose a crew.} For Apollo 11, play Neil Armstrong (commander,
landing and decisions), Buzz Aldrin (LM pilot, budgets and burn pads),
and Michael Collins (CM pilot, timing and rendezvous).
For Apollo 13, play Jim Lovell (commander and manual navigation),
Fred Haise (LM pilot and life support), and Jack Swigert (CM pilot and
power-up). One person can play all roles; two people split the third role.

\textbf{Choose Mission Control.} A referee keeps the answer key hidden,
reads each situation aloud, grades committed sheets, and narrates
checkpoint consequences. In solo play, cover the answer panel until
you commit your sheet; then uncover only that card.

\textbf{Mission order.} Finish cards 11-01 through 11-10 for the beginner
game or 13-01 through 13-10 for the advanced game. Launch and intervening
coasts are narrated unless a card asks for a calculation. The cards
sample the essential game operations; they do not replace hundreds
of actual flight-plan procedures.

\textbf{The role-play loop.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The referee announces GET (ground elapsed time since launch),
the scene, and the supplied telemetry.
\item The acting pilot reads the problem aloud. Another pilot estimates
the order of magnitude and checks units; rotate these jobs each card.
\item Work products, quotients, roots, and logarithms on the slide rule.
Use pencil for sums, differences, clock arithmetic, and decisions.
Write your scale choices and estimate on the card.
\item Agree on the numerical outputs, signed command, and GO/NO-GO
decision. Say ``commit'' and record them. Corrections before commitment
are free; the first committed sheet is the checkpoint.
\item Mission Control grades, applies one reserve charge, and narrates
the consequence. Correct an AMBER or RED sheet before continuing.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{No hidden arithmetic.} All inputs and equations needed for the
game appear on the cards or reference sheet. A player's scientific
knowledge is not used to add secret hazards, additional fuel charges,
or unstated penalties.

\section*{Checkpoints: errors have consequences}
\textbf{Command reserve \(R\)} measures the team's capacity for
corrective work, not propellant, hours, or human endurance.
Apollo 11 begins at \(\boxed{R=6}\); Apollo 13 at \(\boxed{R=4}\).
Reserve is never restored. Physical budgets on the cards remain
separate from this abstract track.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lXl@{}}
\toprule
Band & First committed sheet & Charge\\
\midrule
GREEN (G) & Every output is in its GREEN band and every decision gate
is correct. Execute the nominal command. & 0\\
AMBER (A) & At least one AMBER output, no RED output or failed gate.
Narrate that card's warning, then correct. & 1\\
RED (R) & Any RED output, wrong sign/quadrant, incompatible or missing
units, missing requested output, or wrong discrete decision.
Narrate the card's contingency, then correct. & 2\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\textbf{Grade the worst result once.} Ordinary numerical outputs use
\[
\epsilon=100\,\frac{|q_{\rm player}-q_{\rm reference}|}
{|q_{\rm reference}|}.
\]
GREEN is \(\epsilon\leq1\%\); AMBER is \(1\%<\epsilon\leq5\%\);
RED is \(>5\%\). Referees compare against guard-digit answers, not
the rounded display value. They may use a calculator privately.
Three significant figures are normally sufficient.

\textbf{Clock arithmetic.} For h:m:s event times or intervals, GREEN
means absolute error at most 1 s, AMBER over 1 s through 60 s,
RED over 60 s. These bands do not apply to burn durations computed by
division: those use the ordinary relative bands. Decimal hours computed
from a clock interval also use ordinary bands.

\textbf{Angles.} For 11-10, 13-08, and 13-09, GREEN is within
\(0.05^\circ\), AMBER over \(0.05^\circ\) through \(0.20^\circ\),
RED beyond. Cartridge / station counts and profile / diversion choices
must be exact. Signed negative remainders are valid results when
evaluating a rejected profile. If a reference were zero, require the
correct zero and units rather than dividing by zero.

\textbf{Decision gates.} A chosen option that violates a stated fuel,
oxygen, water, capacity, energy, timing, or entry constraint is RED,
even when the individual numerical errors are small. An otherwise
correct choice supported by mildly inaccurate numbers is graded
AMBER, not automatically RED. Evaluate resource inequalities before
rounding. A rounded bound that exactly equals a rounded load is not
permission to consume reserve.

\textbf{Correction protocol.} Subtract the charge. If \(R\geq0\),
apply the card's narrative and have the crew recompute with a referee
hint if needed. Revisions are not charged again. Show a corrected,
GREEN-quality pad and a valid gate before advancing.
Restore the card's nominal baseline after correction; no numerical
state changes occur beyond recorded reserve and narrative flags.
This prevents a small slide-rule error from becoming an unplayable
cascade. The clock advances to the next card's supplied epoch.

\textbf{If \(R<0\), stop immediately.} Do not solve later cards to
declare a rescue. Use the ending rules on the next page.

\section*{Mission paths and endings}
\begin{panel}{Apollo 11 / beginner}
Launch and Earth parking orbit \(\rightarrow\) TLI (11-01)
\(\rightarrow\) docking with Eagle and stage avoidance (11-02)
\(\rightarrow\) translunar coast and lunar capture (11-03)
\(\rightarrow\) orbit planning (11-04)
\(\rightarrow\) undock and DOI (11-05)
\(\rightarrow\) powered descent (11-06)
\(\rightarrow\) surface EVA (11-07)
\(\rightarrow\) ascent and rendezvous (11-08)
\(\rightarrow\) LM jettison and TEI (11-09)
\(\rightarrow\) entry and recovery (11-10).
\end{panel}

Complete all ten corrected gates with \(R\geq0\):
\textbf{crew recovered; landing achieved}. If any 11-07 warning occurred,
add ``reduced science'' or ``EVA curtailed'' to the ending.
Otherwise record ``full planned science.'' Surviving a warning does not
erase it. The final reserve reports command quality, not human survival odds.

If reserve drops below zero on 11-01 or 11-02, record
\textbf{safe Earth-orbit abort}. On 11-03 through 11-06,
record \textbf{landing canceled; safe-return contingency ending}.
These are narrated training branches, not computed return trajectories.
On 11-07 through 11-10, record
\textbf{simulation terminated; return or rendezvous unresolved}.
Do not pretend a landed crew has an automatic rescue.

\begin{panel}{Apollo 13 / advanced}
Narrate launch, docking, and outbound coast.
At 055:54:53 the oxygen-tank accident occurs (13-01).
Restore free return (13-02), plan oxygen and power (13-03--04),
perform PC+2 (13-05), budget water (13-06),
adapt CO\(_2\) cartridges (13-07), correct the trajectory manually
(13-08--09), and reactivate Odyssey for recovery (13-10).
\end{panel}

Complete all ten corrected gates with \(R\geq0\):
\textbf{all three crew recovered; lunar landing abandoned}.
Any reserve exhaustion means \textbf{rescue simulation failed;
crew survival not established}. Do not invent a successful landing or
safe rescue as consolation. The actual Apollo 13 crew returned safely;
game failure is not an alternate historical claim.

\textbf{Campaign play.} Finish Apollo 11, then start Apollo 13 with its
own fresh reserve of 4. Do not carry reserve or resources between missions.
An optional practice session may waive charges, but it is not a scored run.

\textbf{Resource accounting.} Apollo 13 cards 13-03, 13-04, and 13-06
independently cover the same full return horizon from 058:00:00.
They are planning audits, not new inventory snapshots taken at each
later checkpoint. Do not subtract elapsed-flight consumption again.
Cards 13-01, 13-02, 13-05, and 13-10 explicitly use separate pools.
Cards 13-07--09 supply their own local scenarios.

\section*{The slide-rule flight desk}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
Operation & Hand procedure\\
\midrule
\(ab\), \(a/b\) & C/D: align the C index with \(a\) on D for multiplication;
read D under \(b\) on C. For division align \(b\) on C with \(a\) on D
and read under the C index. Choose left or right index to stay on-scale.\\
\(a^2,\sqrt a\) & D to A gives a square; A to D gives a square root.
Choose the A-scale half using the exponent / an order-of-magnitude estimate.\\
Vector length & Square components on A or C/D, add on paper,
then take a square root. Signs disappear only inside the magnitude;
retain them on the commanded vector.\\
\(10^x\), \(\log_{10}a\) & L reads the logarithmic mantissa associated
with D. Keep the characteristic (integer power of ten) on paper.
For a small negative exponent, use \(10^{-x}=10^{1-x}/10\).\\
Trigonometry & T gives \(\tan\theta\); slide designs differ, so consult
the rule's scale labels. If no T scale, use the table below.
Linear interpolation is pencil arithmetic with C/D division.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{panel}{Decimal places are your responsibility}
A slide rule reads significant figures, not powers of ten.
Example: \(120\text{ m}/2\text{ m/s}\) must be near 60 s,
not 6 or 600 s. Write the estimate first.
Products and quotients require a recorded slide-rule scale operation
to count as completed game work. A correct guessed number is not a
completed calculation sheet; work it before committing.
\end{panel}

\textbf{Common constants and equations}
\[
1\text{ ft}=0.3048\text{ m},\qquad 1\text{ h}=60\text{ min}=3600\text{ s}
\]
\[
\Delta v=\sqrt{\sum_i\Delta v_i^2},\quad
\bar a=\Delta v/t,\quad E=Pt,\quad m=r t,\quad
\Delta v=v_e\ln(m_0/m_f).
\]
\(\ln x=2.302585\log_{10}x\). The rocket equation describes an ideal
single burn without external forces; use only the card's stated model.
Every rate must share the time unit of its duration.

\textbf{Clock example.} GET 102:35:13 means 102 hours after launch,
not 10:35 a.m. Add 11:58 as minutes:seconds to get 102:47:11.

\section*{Apollo 11: the flight plan becomes a game}
The supplied \texttt{Apollo11\_Flight\_Plan.pdf} is NASA's
\emph{Final Flight Plan, July 1, 1969}; \texttt{flight\_plan.txt}

The game's chronology follows its planned sequence, not a reconstructed

\textbf{Start condition.} Crew: Armstrong / Aldrin / Collins.
Command reserve 6. Launch is successful. Write your reserve on the
Apollo 11 ledger before card 11-01.

\textbf{Why the decimal precision varies.} Burn tables provide tenths
of a second, while some summary charts round to a minute.
The game uses burn tables for ignition/duration and the detailed
timeline for entry interface and its elapsed-time table for splashdown.
The summary's 195:17 splashdown is rounded to the minute.

\textbf{What the game does not calculate.} The full launch guidance,
midcourse orbit determination, landmark tracking, P52 realignment,
complete lunar rendezvous, and parachute mechanics are narrated.
The ten cards contain all mandatory hand calculations for this
game's mission, not all computations made by NASA.
